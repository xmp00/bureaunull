The brief — Katerina Volkova

Katerina Volkova shot fine-art portraits and editorial commissions, and her website was quietly costing her work. Built on Flash, it did not exist on an iPhone — which is where art directors and magazine editors first look at a photographer. Images loaded slowly, nothing was shareable, and client proofing happened over email with numbered JPEG attachments and week-long confusion about which frames the client had chosen.

The brief was precise. First, a portfolio where the image is the interface: no chrome, no clutter, a masonry grid that respects different aspect ratios and loads progressively on slow connections. Second, technical credibility — she wanted EXIF data visible for the editors who care about craft. Third, password-protected client galleries where a sitter could view proofs, mark favourites, and download finals — replacing the email-attachment workflow entirely. Everything hand-built; she explicitly did not want a template site that looked like every other photographer's.

// what we built

What we built for Katerina Volkova

01

Lazy-loading masonry grid

A hand-written JavaScript masonry layout that streams images progressively, respects native aspect ratios, and keeps first paint under 400KB even with 60+ portfolio images on the page.

02

EXIF metadata display

Camera, lens, aperture and film-stock notes surfaced in an unobtrusive overlay — a small detail that signalled craft to editors and fellow photographers.

03

Password-protected client galleries

Per-client proofing galleries with a favourites system: clients star their selections, the photographer receives a structured selection list, and final retouched files are delivered through the same private space.

04

Print & booking enquiry flow

A minimal enquiry path separating commission requests from print sales, so editorial leads and fine-art print buyers each reached the right inbox with the right context.

// the result

The result

<400KB

First-paint page weight despite a full portfolio grid — the site scored faster than 95% of photographer sites built on template platforms that year.

3 days

Average client selection turnaround, down from roughly two weeks of email ping-pong over numbered JPEG attachments.

+45%

Year-on-year increase in commission enquiries after launch, with mobile traffic — previously zero — becoming the largest single source of new contacts.
