Objections

We have heard them all.

We are too small to be targeted.
43% of all attacks target small businesses. Bots do not check your turnover. They scan every IP on the internet, 24/7, for known weaknesses. You are not too small — you are exactly the right size: big enough to pay, small enough to be unguarded.
Our hosting provider handles security.
Hosting secures the server. Your application — the login forms, the contact form, the checkout — is your responsibility. SQL injection and broken authentication happen at code level, where hosting ends and your liability begins.
We had a penetration test last year.
Good — you are ahead of 90% of your competitors. But every update, plugin and new feature changes your attack surface. Annual testing is the floor, not the ceiling — and it is exactly what GDPR Art. 32 means by "regular testing".
Security audits are too expensive.
Enterprise audits are. Ours start at €1,500 — because we do not feed an enterprise. Against an average breach cost of $3.31M (IBM 2025, sub-500 staff), that is insurance priced at 0.05% of the risk. Expensive is discovering the breach after your clients do.
We do not collect sensitive data.
You have contact forms, email addresses, invoices — and a reputation. A defaced homepage or a hijacked email account costs you clients even when no data is stolen. Your brand is the asset. The audit protects it.
We will do it later.
The average breach takes 194 days just to identify (IBM 2024). "Later" is the most expensive word in cybersecurity — the attackers' timeline does not wait for yours. An audit this quarter costs €1,500; a breach this quarter costs everything above.
Our developer says the site is secure.
They may be right — and a second pair of adversary-trained eyes is how you prove it. Builders and breakers are different skill sets; that is why the industry separates them. Your developer keeps their job. You keep your business.
We have a firewall and Cloudflare.
Excellent — perimeter tools stop the lazy attacks. They do nothing for broken access control, business-logic abuse, or the plugin vulnerability published last Tuesday. A WAF is a fence. We check whether the building has doors.
Nobody knows we exist. Why would they find us?
They already have. Scanners do not browse — they enumerate. Your IP sits in ranges scanned thousands of times a day, whether you rank on Google or not. Obscurity is not a strategy; it is a delay.
What if you find nothing? Do we still pay?
Yes — and a clean report is a deliverable, not a disappointment. It is the documented due diligence your insurer, your enterprise clients and the DPC all ask for. In sixteen years, we have never delivered an empty report.
